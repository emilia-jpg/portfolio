Building Beliefs

Design Investigations Solo Project Winter 2024

Tutors: Anab Jain, Nikolas Heep, Stefan Zinell, Matthias Pfeffer, Friedrich Schadow

“Building Beliefs” is a cooperative board game that challenges players to balance individual as well as collective goals. Exploring people’s susceptibility to alternative living arrangements, the game confronts them with their motivations to cooperate.

Within the spirit of Vienna’s rich social housing history, players must build a new community by constructing infrastructure and living spaces that fulfill their character’s personal needs. A dynamic scale determines whether player’s beliefs lean toward individualism or cooperation, shaping and changing the game’s winning conditions and available resources.

Exposed to different challenges throughout the game, players must decide whether to collaborate or prioritise individual success, testing their ability to align efforts and overcome obstacles. The final outcome depends on resource management, adaptability, and the willingness to work together in order to achieve shared and personal objectives.

The game was created as part of the "Games" briefing, in which students were asked to develop cooperative games. Within this framework, I found it exciting to find a mechanic that confronts and challenges players with their own motivations and beliefs around collaboration.

A major challenge in developing the game was not making the game conditions too easy. If it's too simple and obvious that cooperation is the best strategy, the course of the game becomes rather predictable and doesn't encourage much reflection. The "Belief Scale" was my solution to this problem. It determines how much a character's beliefs lean, on a scale from 1–12, toward either cooperative or individualistic.

Why Viennese Gemeindebau (social housing)?

Inspired by the book "Everyday Utopia" by Kristen R. Ghodsee, my goal from the start was to engage people on a personal level and reflect on what our lives could look like if we placed collective wellbeing above individual success. Historically, Viennese Gemeindebauten have always pursued exactly that, even though, unfortunately, not as much of that awareness remains today as during the heyday of "Red Vienna“. With shared and accessible infrastructure — such as childcare or healthcare facilities — needs are met and work is divided more fairly; for example, relieving women of unfairly distributed reproductive labor was always an important focus here. Shared resources also prove to be more economical and sustainable. Especially in times of climate change, it's therefore important to think about housing holistically and communally. That's why an important part of "Building Beliefs" is also resource management and dealing with the surrounding nature.

The design of my game pieces was also inspired by the mosaics found on many older Gemeindebauten. Historically, Viennese housing construction placed great value on supporting artists, investing a percentage of the budget in these artworks through "Kunst am Bau" (art in architecture/public art on buildings).
